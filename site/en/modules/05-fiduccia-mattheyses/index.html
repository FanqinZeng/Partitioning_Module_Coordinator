Fiduccia–Mattheyses bisection

The highest gain must also be legal

Move one node at a time. Filter by balance before ranking, locking and rollback.

Original contribution: stephQ-lab · Issue #13
Prerequisites: Lesson 02, Lesson 04

01 / LEARNING OBJECTIVES

What you will learn

  1. Compute single-node move gains
  2. Check post-move balance first
  3. Maintain locks and affected gains
  4. Compute the best move prefix
  5. Explain net gains using pin counts
  6. Separate this teaching implementation from the linear-time claim

02 / PROBLEM CONTRACT

State the problem first

Input
A small undirected weighted graph with positive vertex areas.
Output
One part label per vertex and a replayable event trace.
Objective
Evaluate or reduce crossing edge weight.
Constraints
Use the declared count/balance contract; report counts and areas separately.
Assumptions
Each edge once, deterministic ties; no physical routing simulation.
Stop
No positive prefix or pass limit

03 / BEFORE YOU READ

Terms to know

Why might the person most eager to leave the lift have to wait?

合法候选 / legal move

Both sides remain nonempty and count difference is at most maxDiff.

FS

Number of pins of a net on the moving node's from side.

TE

Number of pins of a net on the target side.

gain bucket

Candidates indexed by gain; this implementation uses a Map and scans legal nodes.

回退 / rollback

Undo every trial after the best prefix.

01 · Why move one vertex?

FM tries one vertex at a time, reducing the candidate set and enabling gain maintenance through net pin counts. A move changes side counts, so balance must be part of legality. The basic lab requires nonempty sides and |n0−n1|≤maxDiff, initially 1.

For AE | BCD, counts are 2 and 3. A and E both have gain 6, but either move produces counts 1 and 4 and is illegal. D has gain 3 and produces counts 3 and 2. D is the correct first choice.

02 · Filter before ranking

For each unlocked vertex, check the assignment after its hypothetical move. Rank only legal candidates, using maximum gain then name order. The table retains blocked candidates and the reason they were excluded.

Moving D gives ADE | BC and cut 9. A can now move legally, giving DE | ABC, counts 2 and 3, and gain 6. Legality changes with the state: a blocked first move may become legal later.

03 · Gains for edges and nets

For ordinary edges, gain is external minus internal weight. In a cut-net hypergraph model, a multi-pin net contributes its weight once whenever pins occupy both sides. Replacing it with pairwise edges generally changes the objective.

Let F be the vertex's current side and T its destination. If the vertex is the only F-side pin of a net, its move removes that net from the cut: add w. If T has no pin, the move makes the net cross: subtract w. Sum these FS/TE contributions over incident nets.

The shared interface accepts ordinary graphs, treating edges as two-pin nets. The underlying FM engine maintains net counts and updates affected gains. The interface does not claim to expose a hypergraph editor that has not been implemented.

04 · Trials, locks and the best prefix

Lock each tried vertex until the next pass. Update gains after the move instead of reusing the initial ranking. When no legal unlocked vertex remains, choose the best cumulative prefix, including the empty prefix.

G01's best first-pass prefix moves D then A, gains 9 in total, and accepts cut 3. Later trials may worsen the state; they do not alter the acceptance rule. The candidate, lock and prefix panels show computed engine state.

If maxDiff=0 with equal counts, every single move breaks equality. FM has no legal move, while KL pair exchanges can still proceed. This is a consequence of the neighborhood and constraint, not a certificate of global optimality.

05 · Area balance is a separate rule

This basic FM engine constrains counts, not areas. Legal counts can coexist with very unequal areas. Adding area bounds requires checking them after every candidate move and validating the initial assignment.

Change maxDiff in the lab. Widening it can make a high-gain vertex eligible earlier. Tightening it can invalidate the initial split. That result is reported as invalid rather than silently widening the rule.

06 · Be precise about linear time

The classic linear-per-pass result depends on the appropriate bucket structure, update rules and cost model. This teaching implementation indexes gains with a Map but scans legal unlocked vertices and records full candidate tables. It does not claim the paper's O(P) pass bound.

Verify semantic invariants: recorded gain equals actual cut reduction, locks do not repeat, trial assignments obey balance, and prefix acceptance never worsens the pass start. A faster implementation must preserve these properties.

07 · Correct the shorthand

“Choose maximum gain” means maximum gain among legal, unlocked candidates. “Move one vertex” does not mean accepting only individually positive moves. KL and FM share the prefix idea while differing in move objects and legality.

Implement move_gain and best_prefix in Python, checking against actual cut changes. Spectral bisection next takes a different approach: use the whole graph's matrix structure to produce an ordering.

08 / COMMON MISTAKES

Conclusions to examine carefully

Highest gain always goes first

A gains 6 illegally; D gains 3 legally.

Compute gains once

After D moves, A becomes legal; update the state.

FM needs no best prefix

Roll back to the maximum cumulative prefix after a pass.

A Map implies linear time

This implementation scans candidates and makes no classic linear-pass claim.
