Recursive bisection

01 / LEARNING OBJECTIVES

What you will learn

  1. Select the largest splittable part
  2. Build the correct induced subgraph
  3. Solve afresh on each subgraph
  4. Account for the full-graph cut
  5. Handle k that is not a power of two
  6. Separate local balance from final capacity

02 / PROBLEM CONTRACT

State the problem first

Input
A small undirected weighted graph with positive vertex areas.
Output
One part label per vertex and a replayable event trace.
Objective
Evaluate or reduce crossing edge weight.
Constraints
Use the declared count/balance contract; report counts and areas separately.
Assumptions
Each edge once, deterministic ties; no physical routing simulation.
Stop
At k parts or no legal split

03 / BEFORE YOU READ

Terms to know

When two teams split into smaller teams, do the old boundaries still count?

诱导子图 / induced subgraph

The chosen vertices and all edges with both endpoints in that set.

叶子 / leaf

A current part that can still be split.

分裂树 / split tree

A record of each parent part and its two children.

新增割 / added cut

The increase in full-graph cut caused by a split.

局部约束 / local constraint

A rule for the current split, not a guarantee on every final part.

01 · Produce k parts using a bisection solver

Recursive bisection repeatedly splits one current part until there are k parts. k need not be a power of two. Starting from one part, each successful split adds one, so reaching k needs k−1 splits.

This course selects the part with the most vertices, breaking ties by smaller label, then runs spectral bisection afresh on its induced subgraph. Largest means count, not area. Every vertex belongs to exactly one current part.

02 · What belongs in the induced subgraph?

For selected vertices S, retain only edges whose endpoints both lie in S. Edges to other parts already contribute to the full-graph cut. Including them in the local degree matrix would change the subproblem.

G01 first splits into DE | ABC with cut 3. The next solver uses only AB:5, AC:1 and BC:4 inside ABC. It splits AB | C, adding AC+BC=5. The final AB | C | DE assignment has full-graph cut 8.

03 · The split tree and labels

Record each parent, its children, local ordering and added cut. Labels identify parts; they do not measure quality. Swapping side names or renumbering parts leaves the grouping unchanged, so canonical comparisons sort members and ignore labels.

Every split in this lab is recomputed. Change the graph, weights or k and rewind the resulting tree. Results are generated by an algorithm rather than a fixed assignment/history table.

04 · Account for the full cut

A split only separates vertices within its parent part. Previously crossing edges remain crossing. Therefore pure splitting cannot reduce the old cut; the increment is exactly the parent-internal weight newly separated by this split.

G01 follows 0→3→8. Reporting only the last subgraph cut 5 omits earlier boundaries. Recompute full-graph cut at every event and compare it with the accumulated increments.

This monotonicity belongs to the construction stage. Later cross-part swaps can change old boundaries and reduce cut.

05 · Local balance is not final capacity

Requiring each child to contain at least 20% of its parent's vertices does not guarantee equally sized final parts. A 3/2 split followed by 2/1 yields counts 2/1/2. Unequal vertex areas can widen the disparity.

Final capacity bounds each part's area and needs a separate check. An infeasible result is not a successful low-cut solution. Guaranteeing a final capacity requires a strategy aware of remaining parts and area budgets; this basic constructor makes no such claim.

06 · Termination and failure

Stop at k nonempty parts, with k no larger than the vertex count. If a selected subgraph has no prefix satisfying the declared window, report infeasible rather than silently using an illegal midpoint.

Before reaching k there is a non-singleton part, but the chosen window can still prevent its split. Expose that constraint failure and let the learner deliberately change the parameters.

07 · Implement and refine

Implement induced_subgraph and largest_part in Python. Preserve original internal edges and weights; select by count then label. The visualizer shows the subgraph chosen by your functions. The bisection solver was examined separately in Lesson 6.

Recursive bisection reuses a simple solver, but pure splitting cannot reunite endpoints separated earlier. The next lesson adds legal cross-part exchanges to test whether construction can be improved.

08 / COMMON MISTAKES

Conclusions to examine carefully

k must be a power of two

Split one part at a time; k=3 needs two splits.

Cropping the old order is a fresh solve

The induced matrix changes and requires a new solve.

Use only the last subgraph cut

Add the new 5 to the old 3 for full-graph cut 8.

Local balance guarantees capacity

Local count fractions differ from final area capacity.
