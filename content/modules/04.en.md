## 01 · Preserve counts with a pair

KL starts from a complete split and exchanges one unlocked vertex from each side. Counts remain fixed; areas need not. Our initial AE | BCD assignment has cut 12. To rank exchanges, first measure each vertex's local contribution.

Let D(v)=external(v)−internal(v). External edges lead to the other side, internal edges to the same side. D is the gain of moving one vertex, but a pair exchange needs a correction.

## 02 · The pair-gain correction

For opposite-side a and b, g(a,b)=D(a)+D(b)−2w(a,b). Use zero when no edge joins them. Adding the D values counts the a–b edge as removed twice. In reality, after swapping both endpoints it remains crossing, so subtract 2w.

For AE | BCD, D(A)=6, D(E)=6, D(B)=1, D(C)=−2 and D(D)=3. A↔D gains 6+3=9 and produces DE | ABC with cut 3. Independently verify the formula by copying the assignment, exchanging the pair and recomputing cut_before−cut_after.

## 03 · Locking is not acceptance

After selecting the highest-gain pair, exchange it in the trial assignment and lock both vertices. Recompute candidates on that trial state. Equal gains use a then b in name order, with a drawn from the current side 0.

Continue even when the next gain is negative. G01's first pass has gains 9 and −8, with cumulative gains 9 and 1. The second trial raises cut from 3 to 11. This is not the accepted result. The lab distinguishes trial-swap from best-prefix events.

## 04 · Best prefix and rollback

Include G0=0, the empty prefix, then compute Gk=Σi≤k gi. Choose the earliest prefix with maximum cumulative gain. If no positive cumulative gain exists, retain the empty prefix. Do not pick the largest individual gain or splice together positive moves while skipping their intervening states.

The G01 prefix values are [0,9,1], so retain one exchange. Reapply that prefix from the pass start and discard later trials, accepting cut 3. The graph and metrics must roll back together.

For the illustrative arithmetic sequence [−2,5,−1], the best prefix has length 2 and gain 3. An initially harmful move can lead to a useful sequence. This sequence explains prefix selection; it is not claimed to be a G01 trace.

## 05 · Passes and termination

Unlock vertices and begin the next pass from the accepted assignment. Stop when the empty prefix wins. If the configured pass limit is reached, report limit instead. Neither status establishes a global optimum.

KL explores improvements found by its pair-and-prefix strategy. Matching a tiny exact optimum on one graph does not prove a general guarantee. Because counts stay fixed, pair swaps also cannot repair an inappropriate initial count target.

## 06 · Four implementation invariants

After every trial, verify fixed side counts, no repeated locked vertices, actual cut difference equal to recorded gain, and cumulative gain equal to start cut minus current trial cut. After acceptance, cut must equal start cut minus best cumulative gain and cannot be worse than the pass start.

```text
repeat a pass:
    trial ← accepted; unlock all
    choose best unlocked cross pair
    swap in trial; lock both; record gain
    repeat until one side has no unlocked vertex
    accept earliest maximum positive prefix
stop when the empty prefix is best
```

The teaching engine enumerates candidate pairs for transparency. Its runtime should not be generalized to every engineered KL implementation.

## 07 · How FM differs

Both KL and FM use trials, locks and a best prefix. It is incorrect to say that only FM tolerates worsening trials. Their important differences concern the moving object, legality checks and candidate maintenance.
